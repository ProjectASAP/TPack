# Reference results — measured vs. the paper

This is the evidence behind the claims table in [`../../docs/AE.md`](../../docs/AE.md).
Each section puts the paper's published value beside what we measured when
re-running the artifact from the published datasets.

**How these were produced.** All parts run with `NUM_SEEDS=1` (and
`UBER_MAX_TRACES=200000` for the scalability sweep) on a CloudLab **c240g5**:
2× Intel Xeon Silver 4114 (40 threads, 2.2 GHz), 187 GB RAM, Ubuntu 24.04.
The paper's evaluation ran on a single workstation with an 8-core AMD Ryzen 7
9700X (16 threads, up to 5.58 GHz) and 64 GB DDR5. **Fidelity is
hardware-independent; cost is not** — the cost model bills measured CPU seconds,
and everything runs slower here, so every cost figure below is higher than the
paper's. This matters for exactly one claim (Finding 4) and is quantified there.

Raw scorecard output is in [`scorecards/`](scorecards/); regenerated figures and
LaTeX tables are in [`figures/`](figures/).

## Finding 1 — cost–fidelity across datasets

Per-metric fidelity (%) of TPack vs. Head 1:50 at comparable cost. Paper values
are from `tables/cross_dataset.tex`; ours from
[`figures/cross_dataset.tex`](figures/cross_dataset.tex), regenerated by
`plot_paper --mode tab_cross_dataset`.

### TPack

| Dataset | Metric | Paper | Measured | Δ |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| OTel Demo | Rate | 67.2 | 67.1 | −0.1 |
| | Error | 95.2 | 95.9 | +0.7 |
| | Duration | 47.5 | 47.2 | −0.3 |
| | Graph | 96.5 | 96.6 | +0.1 |
| RE2-TT | Rate | 94.6 | 94.7 | +0.1 |
| | Duration | 80.7 | 80.7 | 0.0 |
| | Graph | 95.6 | 95.5 | −0.1 |
| RE2-OB | Rate | 99.6 | 99.6 | 0.0 |
| | Error | 95.8 | 96.6 | +0.8 |
| | Duration | 80.2 | 80.2 | 0.0 |
| | Graph | 99.3 | 99.2 | −0.1 |
| Uber (50K) | Rate | 90.8 | 89.9 | −0.9 |
| | Error | 83.2 | 82.3 | −0.9 |
| | Duration | 49.3 | 49.5 | +0.2 |
| | Graph | 96.3 | 95.1 | −1.2 |

**All 15 cells within 1.2 points**, most within 0.5.

### Head 1:50 (baseline)

| Dataset | Rate | Error | Duration | Graph |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| OTel Demo | 6.9 → 6.9 | 0.0 → 0.0 | 8.7 → 8.8 | 65.3 → **59.9** |
| RE2-TT | 41.6 → 41.6 | — | 80.6 → 82.1 | 18.6 → 17.4 |
| RE2-OB | 73.0 → 72.7 | 7.5 → 8.9 | 79.3 → 79.4 | 68.4 → 68.0 |
| Uber (50K) | 47.3 → 47.1 | 3.6 → 3.3 | 50.9 → 51.5 | 62.1 → **75.3** |

The baselines are noisier than TPack. Twelve of sixteen cells land within 1.5
points, but two graph-fidelity cells move a lot: OTel Demo by 5.4 and Uber by
13.2. This is a **single-seed artifact**, and head sampling at 1:50 is exactly
where single-seed variance is worst — a 1-in-50 sample rarely retains both
endpoints of an edge, so which edges survive swings hard between draws. It is
also why the paper averages three seeds. TPack's own numbers, which are what the
Findings are about, do not have this problem: the model is fit to the whole
distribution rather than a sparse sample.

**Verdict: reproduced.** One consequence worth stating: on RE2-TT duration the
paper has TPack ahead by 0.1 points (80.7 vs 80.6) and our run has head 1:50
ahead by 1.4 (82.1 vs 80.7). That flips one bolded cell. A 0.1-point margin is
inside noise either way, and it does not affect Finding 1, which is about mean
fidelity across metrics.

### Model size — does not reproduce

| | Paper | Measured |
|---|---|---|
| otel-demo model (gzipped protobuf) | ~520 KB | **769 KB** |
| Compression vs. raw | ~130× | ~106× (vs. 81 MB gzipped OTLP) |

The model size is a direct, unambiguous comparison and it is **48% larger** than
published. The compression ratio depends on what you measure the model against,
so treat the 769 KB as the solid number and the ratio as derived. Both terms
differ and not by a common factor, so this is not a units or scaling mismatch.
We cannot reconcile it from the published dataset and are flagging it rather than
leaving it to be found; see commit `dfa4859`. Finding 1 itself makes no claim
about model size, and ~106× supports the same conclusion as ~130×.

## Finding 2 — unit cost falls with ingestion rate

Paper `tables/scalability.tex` vs. [`figures/scalability.tex`](figures/scalability.tex).
Uber is remapped into a single 60-second bucket, so the trace count *is* the
ingestion rate. "Time" is compression + decompression.

| Traces | Spans (P → M) | Raw gz (P → M) | Model gz (P → M) | Ratio (P → M) | Time s (P → M) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 10K | 10M → 10M | 232 MB → 230 MB | 9 MB → 9 MB | 26× → 26× | 4 → 12 |
| 20K | 19M → 19M | 467 MB → 465 MB | 13 MB → 13 MB | 36× → 36× | 8 → 22 |
| ~50K | 48M → 47M | 1.1 GB → 1.1 GB | 22 MB → 22 MB | 53× → 52× | 16 → 41 |
| 100K | 94M → 94M | 2.3 GB → 2.3 GB | 32 MB → 32 MB | 71× → 71× | 29 → 74 |
| 200K | 187M → **186M** | 4.5 GB → 4.5 GB | 47 MB → 47 MB | 95× → **96×** | 56 → 133 |
| 474K | 441M | 11 GB | 76 MB | 140× | 122 |

Every size column reproduces essentially exactly. Model sizes are identical at
all five points; raw sizes agree within 1%; compression ratios agree within one.

Time is **2.4–3.0× slower** across every row, consistently. TPack appears not to
be tuned for this machine (see Finding 4 below); what it produces is unchanged.

The 474K row was not run (`UBER_MAX_TRACES=200000`); it is the most expensive
point in the sweep by a wide margin. The paper's headline rate comes from the
200K row, which we do reproduce: **186.2M spans inside one 60-second epoch**
against the paper's 187M spans/min, 0.4% apart.

Unit cost falls monotonically across the sweep, 1.03 µs/span at 10K to 0.55
µs/span at 200K — a 1.9× improvement — which is the claim itself. Compression
also rises with scale (26× → 96×) as a larger epoch amortizes the model's fixed
structure over more spans.

**Verdict: reproduced.**

### A note on the Uber subset

`--remap` discards traces whose root span exceeds 60 s, then subsamples to
`--max-traces`. Until recently that subsample drew from a Go map without sorting
first, so the selected subset — and with it the span count — shifted by up to ~1%
between runs even at a fixed seed. That is fixed in
`cmd/tpack-eval/format_converter.go`, and transforms are now reproducible. **The
numbers above were measured before the fix**, so a fresh run lands on a slightly
different subset; expect sub-1% movement in span counts and correspondingly small
movement in derived figures.

## Finding 3 — fidelity by query type

From [`scorecards/otel-demo.txt`](scorecards/otel-demo.txt), TPack on otel-demo:

| Metric | Score |
|---|---|
| Rate | 67.1 |
| Error | 95.9 |
| Duration | 47.2 |
| Graph | 96.6 |

The ordering is the claim, and it holds: error and graph are strong, **duration
is TPack's weakest category**, exactly as the paper states — the timing model is
deliberately simple (a GMM for root durations, regression for children). Compare
against Head 1:50 in the Finding 1 table above: 8.8 on duration, 0.0 on error.

**Verdict: reproduced.**

## Finding 4 — RCA utility on RE2-OB — partial

AC@1 on RE2-OB (7 services) and AC@4 on RE2-TT (27 services), chosen so both have
a ~15% random-guess baseline. 90 scenarios each.

### RE2-OB, AC@1

| TPack | head 1:1 | 1:2 | 1:3 | 1:4 | 1:5 | 1:10 | sifter | tail / hindsight | 1:20 | 1:50 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **66.7** | 60.0 | 58.9 | 54.4 | 54.4 | 51.1 | 47.8 | 45.6 | 43.3 | 38.9 | 31.1 |

| Sub-claim | Paper | Measured | |
|---|---|---|---|
| RCA accuracy on RE2-OB | > 60% | **66.7%** | ✔ |
| Beats every head-sampling rate | yes | best head is 1:1 at **60.0%** | ✔ |
| Cost vs. head 1:1 | 50× cheaper | **12.8× cheaper** | ✘ |

### Why the cost ratio misses

Mean per scenario across the 90 RE2-OB scenarios, computed with the same
`ReportParser` the figures use:

| Approach | Transmitted | Transmission $ | Compute $ | Total $ |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| TPack | 0.1448 MiB | 1.414e−5 | **7.217e−5** | 8.631e−5 |
| head 1:1 | 11.1193 MiB | 1.086e−3 | 1.472e−5 | 1.101e−3 |
| **Ratio** | **76.8×** | 76.8× | 0.2× | **12.8×** |

**84% of TPack's cost on this machine is CPU**, because the cost model bills
measured CPU seconds and head sampling pays essentially none — it is a
pass-through. So the ratio is dominated by our slower cores.

Counting only bytes shipped, which does not depend on the hardware, TPack sends
**76.8× less data** than head 1:1. So the paper's 50× sits between our two
measurements: 12.8× with compute billed as we measured it, 76.8× with compute
ignored entirely.

Everything ran substantially slower on the c240g5 — 2.4–3.0× on the scalability
sweep, more than the clock difference alone predicts. We think TPack is simply
not tuned for this machine: it was developed against a fast single-socket desktop
CPU, while the c240g5 is a dual-socket Xeon with slower cores, so there is likely
a CPU or memory bottleneck we have not chased down. We report what we measured;
we cannot confirm 50× on this hardware.

### RE2-TT, AC@4 — the paper's stated exception

| head 1:1 | 1:2 | 1:4 | 1:5 | 1:3 | **TPack** | 1:500 | tail / hindsight | 1:10 | 1:50 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 72.2 | 61.1 | 57.8 | 57.8 | 56.7 | **53.3** | 42.2 | 36.7 | 34.4 | 23.3 |

Head 1:1 leads by 18.9 points and TPack sits just under head 1:5, closely
matching the paper's own characterisation that on RE2-TT "head 1:1 leads by ~17
points but TPack still matches head 1:5" at several times lower cost. Finding 4
is scoped to RE2-OB, and that is where it holds.

**Verdict: partial** — the accuracy claim and the beats-all-rates claim
reproduce; the 50× cost figure does not on this hardware.

## Finding 5a — node-level modeling

`node-ablation` on otel-demo: 22 leave-one-out drops of each primary attribute
plus one "add-one-in" that promotes `net.peer.port` (a high-cardinality dependent
attribute) into the primary set. 24 variants total, in
[`figures/node_ablation.pdf`](figures/node_ablation.pdf).

The claim is directional — dropping key attributes hurts the tradeoff, and adding
a high-cardinality one also hurts — and it holds. The four most damaging drops are
`operation.name`, `service.name`, `status.code` and `http.target`, matching the
paper's A–D variants; the remaining 18 cluster near the unmodified configuration.

**Verdict: reproduced.**

## Finding 5b — graph-level modeling

`graph-ablation` across six Uber sizes, edge-probability model vs. template
dictionary. Data in
[`fig13_graph_ablation.json`](fig13_graph_ablation.json), plotted in
[`figures/graph_ablation.pdf`](figures/graph_ablation.pdf).

| Sizes | 1K → 50K traces |
|---|---|
| Edge-model fidelity | 71.0 → 79.2 |
| Template fidelity | ~88.7 → ~89 |
| Templates | 995 → 38,523 |
| Distinct edges | 59,516 → 233,215 |

Both halves of the paper's claim hold. Template memorization buys higher fidelity
but at higher cost, and it **scales worse**: template count grows roughly linearly
in trace count (995 → 38,523, a 39× increase over a 50× range) while distinct
edges grow only 3.9×. That is exactly the asymmetry the paper argues makes edge
modeling the right choice at scale. Edge-model cost per billion spans falls
0.483 → 0.340 → 0.234 across the sweep.

**Verdict: reproduced.**
